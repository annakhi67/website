Anmeldung
Assistant

A guide for newcomers to Berlin: say what you need to sort out, then get the documents, appointment slots and a ready form draft for it.

Role UX/UI designer, soloTime October 2026Tools HTML/CSS/JS prototypeType Concept project
01Problem

Registering in Berlin is confusing before it is difficult

Anyone who moves to Berlin has to register their address (Anmeldung) within 14 days. The process is known for German terms, a key document most newcomers have never heard of, and appointments that are hard to find. As someone who studies and lives in Germany, I wanted to see how much of the stress a clear guide could remove.

Honest scope. This is a concept. I did not run interviews or tests for it. The needs below are assumptions drawn from official sources, and I mark what I would verify with real newcomers. The prototype is not an official service. The appointment slots and the saved personal data in the prototype are examples.
02Facts

What the official sources say

I read the official Berlin information first, so the content is based on facts, not memory:

  • Registration is due within 14 days of moving in, but appointments are scarce, so in practice many people register later.
  • The landlord confirmation (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung) is required. A rental contract does not replace it. It lists the landlord, the move-in date, the address, the names of everyone registering and a signature.
  • You also need a valid passport or ID and the registration form. At a first registration in Berlin, marriage or birth certificates may be requested.
  • Registration is in person, not by post. You get the registration certificate (Meldebestätigung) at the appointment if everything is complete.
  • For a student residence permit, the immigration office (LEA) requires a personal visit with an appointment, and a typical document list includes passport, biometric photo, application form, enrolment proof, proof of address, funds and health insurance.

Sources: service.berlin.de (registration), verwaltung.bund.de, service.berlin.de (residence permit). Requirements can change by district, so check the current pages.

03Assumptions

What I assumed about the user

I designed for a typical newcomer: a student or young professional who speaks some German or none, moved in recently, and worries about missing a rule. This is a working assumption, not a researched persona.

German terms are the biggest barrier

Every German word has its English meaning next to it.

05 · Prototype

Try it yourself

The whole app is clickable. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is saved or sent.

  1. Choose a goal and a key date.
  2. Tick documents and see the "Plan to print" note.
  3. Pick an appointment slot (example data).
  4. Tap "Autofill from saved data" in the form, then open the draft.

06Decisions

Design decisions

  • Ask for the goal first. Documents, office, slots and form all depend on what you are registering for, so the app asks this before anything else.
  • Start with one date. It is the only input needed to show the deadline, so the app is useful within seconds.
  • German and English together. The German term stays visible because it is the word on the real form and at the office.
  • A ready message instead of advice. "Ask your landlord" is vague. A copyable German message with the required items removes the hardest step.
  • Draft, not submission. The form stays on paper at the office, so the app creates a draft to copy from and says nothing is sent anywhere. Suggestions come from saved data and are never filled without a tap.
  • Slots in the app. Seeing open slots removes a trip through several websites. The booking itself still happens on the official site.
  • Paper reminders. The office works with paper, so the app says what to print.
  • Calm about the deadline. If the date has passed, the app says to book the earliest slot, instead of a red warning.
  • Accessible colors. Dark teal for actions (white text 6.05:1), and every text pair checked against WCAG AA.
07Next

What I would do next

  • Interview 5 people who registered in the last year, and rewrite the assumptions with their answers.
  • Test the task "prepare for your appointment" and count where people get stuck.
  • Find out whether the booking systems offer open slots in a form an app may use. In this concept the slots are example data. A real product would need an official data source or partnership.
  • Review the content with someone who works at a Bürgeramt, since rules can differ.
  • Add more languages and more situations (a second home, changing district).
